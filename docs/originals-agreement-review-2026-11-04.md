# Originals agreement review — 4 November 2026

This is a researched drafting and implementation review, not a solicitor's opinion or legal sign-off. The scope is the reservation agreement and its relationship to the existing owner agreement, royalty calculation and credit-confirmation flow. No production records or signed agreements were changed.

## Changes made in version 0.2

The reservation dialog no longer displays the countdown-opening sentence, agreement/version banner or private-pilot/legal-review notice. The instruction to use a public/artistic credit name has been removed from the agreement. The consent text no longer says “provisional pilot”. The master allocation now names **TSC (The Supreme Collective)** explicitly. Public/anonymous credit preferences themselves continue to work.

Internal agreement versions, text hashes and acceptance evidence remain. The server now hashes the actual displayed clauses **and the exact acknowledgement sentence**, without the removed banner or an undisplayed review notice. Versions changed to `originals-contributor-v0.2`, `originals-mix-v0.2` and `originals-master-v0.2`. The reservation request must echo the same acknowledgement, so older clients cannot save evidence claiming a new signature sentence they did not display. Earlier accepted evidence remains untouched; revised terms require a fresh affirmative acknowledgement for new reservations/advance activation.

The revised wording addresses:

- Permission to store, audition, align, edit and combine submissions within this project; source downloads only for authorised production use. It is a limited non-exclusive project permission, not an assignment, publishing appointment or release licence.
- Authority to contribute and disclosure of existing publisher, label, sample or other restrictions.
- Confidentiality exceptions for public/prior/independently developed information, confidential professional advice and required/protected disclosure.
- Recorded partial selections and a single contribution share for multiple stems/takes/ranges within the same person's role.
- The difference between master-income percentages and copyright ownership, and the absence of guaranteed fees or earnings.
- No unspecified cost deductions. Collection, accounting and payment arrangements must be agreed before commercial exploitation, rather than pretending an undefined “permitted direct costs” clause is complete.
- Songwriting calculations as proposed allocations requiring agreement, rather than a checkbox deciding authorship or silently transferring a writer's rights.
- Separate statutory performer royalties, and no implied mandate for TSC to collect those payments.
- Expiry/release without automatic forfeiture of accepted, accrued rights; a recorded concerns/retraction process.
- Purpose-limited retention and privacy, no unrelated marketing consent, and no retrospective rewriting of accepted terms.
- An explicit intention to sign electronically using the typed name and affirmative checkbox.

The financial headline remains 20% TSC / 20% owner / 40% contributor pool / 10% mix / 10% mastering. No royalty calculations or existing business decisions were silently changed during this drafting review.

## Subsequent user-confirmed policy — version 0.3

The user confirmed that the Originals contracting business is **The Supreme Collective Ltd, company number 16883956**, and that TSC will collect/pay the royalties it is authorised to collect. They prefer **no expense deductions**, with promotion processes funded by TSC's 20%.

Version 0.3 incorporates those confirmations into the shared contributor/mix/master reservation terms. The master pool is royalty receipts paid or credited to TSC or its appointed distributor for the recording, before TSC/project expenses, rather than platform subscription revenue. TSC funds promotion, administration, artwork, distributor charges, software and ordinary payment/accounting costs from its own 20%. Distributor charges retained at remittance must still be identified and funded from TSC's share. TSC may not reduce the other shares, add another commission or charge an overspend to contributors. The eventual release mandate remains separate from this reservation-stage permission.

**Recommended timetable, not yet an agreed contractual deadline:** twice-yearly statements and payments, for receipts credited during January–June and July–December, payable by 30 September and 31 March respectively. This provides three months to reconcile each half-year and places the processing runs in March and September. It does not mean that a stream in a particular month has already been paid by a platform. No minimum payment threshold or forfeiture of small balances has been added. The user has not yet chosen exact dates; these must not be silently treated as an existing payment obligation or implemented payout system.

The [UK voluntary music-streaming transparency code](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/uk-voluntary-code-of-good-practice-on-transparency-in-music-streaming), sections 1.5–1.7, supports clearly defined revenue/deductions and at least twice-yearly statements and due payments. This is voluntary good-practice guidance, not a claim that annual accounting is universally unlawful. March/September and the three-month reconciliation period are our proposal, not dates prescribed by the code.

**Illustration:** £1,000 of recording royalties credited before distributor/TSC costs gives £200 to TSC, £200 to the owner, £400 to the contributor pool, £100 to mixing and £100 to mastering. If distribution/promotion/administration cost £60, those costs reduce TSC's retained £200 to £140; the other allocations do not change. These are master-income shares only, excluding separately administered songwriting or performer payments.

## Primary sources checked

- [Intellectual Property Office: copyright ownership](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/ownership-of-copyright-works). Ownership depends on the type of work and the circumstances, including commission/employment and joint creation. This supports separating contractual income percentages from ownership and recording the permission needed to use contributed material.
- [GOV.UK: licensing and selling copyright](https://www.gov.uk/copyright/license-and-sell-your-copyright). Licensing and transfer are distinct; copyright transfer requires a signed written agreement. The revision uses a limited development permission instead of implying that reserving assigns copyright.
- [Intellectual Property Office: performers' rights](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/performers-rights/performers-rights). Performers have rights separate from copyright, including controls over uses and moral rights. The revised wording preserves those rights rather than implying a master-income checkbox replaces them.
- [PPL: allocation between performers and recording rightsholders](https://www.ppluk.com/help-centre/faq/how-is-the-money-distributed-between-performers-and-recording-rightsholders-in-the-uk/). PPL's qualifying public-performance/broadcast allocations are distinct from the project's contractual master-income pool. The project's 20/20/40/10/10 framework should not be presented as PPL's performer allocation.
- [PRS for Music: correcting an omitted writer and agreed shares](https://help.prsformusic.com/s/article/why-is-my-name-not-on-the-work-my-bandmate-registered-for-us-all). The registration needs the writer and agreed share, supporting reconciliation and recorded agreement before registration, rather than relying solely on a checkbox default.
- [Law Commission: electronic execution of documents](https://lawcom.gov.uk/project/electronic-execution-of-documents/). For England and Wales, electronic signatures can authenticate a document where signing intention and applicable formalities are satisfied. Typed-name/checkbox evidence supports intention; it does not by itself establish identity, capacity or satisfaction of every special formality.
- [ICO: storage limitation](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-protection-principles/a-guide-to-the-data-protection-principles/storage-limitation/). Personal data must not be held longer than necessary, and retention needs justification and review. The ICO notes this guidance is under review following the Data (Use and Access) Act; retention and privacy operations still need a current review before wider launch.

## Validation and scope

The change is limited to the shared reservation terms, their display and audit-text construction, plus review documentation. It does not silently amend previously signed wording, impose a new royalty rate, complete royalty-payment features, change the credit override or resolve the identified calculator-policy mismatch. Existing reservation acceptance, renewal, identity/role binding and stale-term rejection tests are retained. Browser verification checks the requested removals, named TSC allocation, updated acknowledgement, mobile layout and consent gates.
